Option A · Careers as Distinctions

Careers

Your Career is one of your three Distinctions — rated ⑧, Hinder free, like any other. Each career below offers three authored SFX; choose two at creation. The third is unlockable later with a Milestone. What you did for a living is who you are; the dice have finally stopped pretending otherwise.

Raider

Infiltration · Theft · Dungeon-crawling

The Mines of Glimmōr are your second home. You go in quiet and come out rich, or loud and come out alive.

  • Take the Shaft — When you create an asset related to infiltration, theft, or escape, step it up.
  • Glimmōr Native — Spend a glitter die to step down a frost-dungeon or Mines complication affecting you.
  • Sledgehammer Exit — When you give in during a contest, gain an extra glitter die of any kind. You never leave empty-handed.

Cryomancer

Ice-magic · Branches · The cold itself

You read the weather the way other girls read texts. Your magic grows in branches, and you arrive at the prom already grown.

  • Branch Scholar — Begin with two Cryomancy branches, one at ⑧ and one at ⑥.
  • Cold Against Cold — When a branch’s tag matches a prince’s Undone by tag, step up the effect die against him.
  • Glyphwright — Spend a glitter die to inscribe a branch glyph from memory; no need to find it carved somewhere first.

Socialite

Courts · Dance clubs · The prom itself

You know who wants what from whom before the first song ends. The room is your dungeon.

  • Read the Room — When you enter a dance club, court, or prom scene, ask two questions about who wants what from whom; the GM answers honestly.
  • Never Miss a Step — Accepting a hitch complication in a social scene grants an extra Charm glitter die.
  • Grand Entrance — Arriving mid-scene creates an ⑧ asset describing the impression you make.

Hinder, like any Distinction

Roll your Career as ④ instead of ⑧ and gain a glitter die. Failure is also a kind of entrance.

Witch

Rot-magic · The coven below

The coven below the frost raised you. What rots, you rule.

  • Rot Speaks — Your Rotwork branches carry the Rot tag; when it matches a prince’s Undone by tag, step up the effect die against him.
  • Feed the Rot — Once per scene, include a Glitter-touched (Festering) complication as if it were an asset.
  • Glyph-Scarred — Begin with two Rotwork branches, one at ⑧ and one at ⑥. The scars were the lessons.

Blade

Duels · Bodyguarding · Deniability

Duels at dawn, bodyguarding after dark. The Mercenaries at Makeout Forge taught you the price of everything, and you charge it.

  • Duelist — Step up the effect die in an accepted formal duel.
  • Bodyguard — Spend a glitter die to take a complication intended for an ally.
  • Ending Conversations — When you enter an ongoing contest, step up your first action’s effect die.

Putting it on the sheet

  1. Choose one of the five careers; write it as a Distinction at ⑧.
  2. Choose two of its three SFX; write them beside it.
  3. The third SFX waits behind a Milestone — 5 XP to unlock, once the story has earned it.
Distinctions (fragment)
Raider of the Glimmōr Mines ⑧ — Take the Shaft, Sledgehammer Exit
Hinder free · third SFX (Glimmōr Native) unlockable via Milestone.

Why Distinctions

The cheapest restoration — every career package already exists, Hinder gives each career a built-in failure mode, and the die stays where the drama is.

There are no plot points in Girlville. There is only glitter.

Option B · Careers as Grouped Talents

Career Talents

Talents are unrated packages of narrative permission and SFX that accent your rated traits. A talent never adds a die to your pool — it changes what the dice you already roll are allowed to do. Talents may be grouped by profession, and Girlville has five such groups. Begin with two talents; one of them should be your career.

OPTION C
Magic as Ability Lists

Cryomancy and Rotwork, branch by branch

✦  ❄  ✦
Option C · Magic as Ability Lists

Cryomancy

Cryomancy is an Ability List: each branch is a rated trait with a descriptor tag, effect tags, its own Limit, and unlockable SFX. Begin with two branches — one at ⑧, one at ⑥ — each with one SFX free. New branches open through play (teachers, glyphs); further SFX unlock as the branch grows (⑧, then ⑫).

Heartfrost ⑧/⑥

Attack, Control · Ice

Weaponize emotional cold.

Limit: Cannot harm someone against whom you hold no grudge.

Shatter: Spend a glitter die; step up the effect die on a Heartbreak-marked foe.

Glassveil ⑧/⑥

Defense · Charm

Mirror-ice armor against blades and scrutiny.

Limit: While the veil is active, your Glamour complications step up.

Reflect: Spend a glitter die: the attacker's effect die hits them as a complication.

Snowblind ⑧/⑥

Sensory · Ice

Read the memories carried by snow.

Limit: Visions arrive as weather, not words.

Whiteout Memory: Spend a glitter die; ask the storm about this place's past.

Deepdrift ⑧/⑥

Movement · Ice

Travel through snow and whiteouts.

Limit: Cannot arrive anywhere warmer than the departure point.

Cut Across: Spend a glitter die; arrive mid-scene with an ⑧ Out of Nowhere.

Glint ⑧/⑥

Enhancement · Charm

Lend an ally supernatural shine.

Limit: The target's next failure becomes your social responsibility.

Starlight Loan: Step down Glint for the scene to step up an ally's Glamour die.

Tags do the talking

Ice and Charm are descriptor tags; Attack, Defense, Sensory, Movement, Enhancement are effect tags. Tags unlock SFX and feed prince vulnerabilities — match a prince's Undone by tag and the fiction turns in your favor.

Rotwork

The forbidden twin. Thaw lives here now — it was always Rot-tagged. A witch begins with two Rotwork branches, one at ⑧ and one at ⑥. Everything else works exactly like Cryomancy: teachers, glyphs, growth, SFX at ⑧ and ⑫.

Thaw ⑧/⑥

Control · Rot

Melts, mends, and ends enchantments. The spring the frost fears.

Limit: Growing Dread — both 1s and 2s hitch.

Spring's Price: Spend a glitter die to step down a complication by two steps instead of one on a recovery test.

Fester ⑧/⑥

Attack · Rot

Accelerate rot in wounds, grudges, and reputations.

Limit: Indiscriminate — on a hitch, the rot spreads to something you value; the GM says what.

Feed the Wound: Spend a glitter die to step up the effect die against someone suffering a Rot-tagged complication.

Wither ⑧/⑥

Control · Rot

Sap warmth, strength, and color; leave the target diminished.

Limit: Requires touch — or a sympathetic token freely given. Stolen tokens don't count.

Long Winter: Spend a glitter die to make a Wither complication persist one extra scene.

Hex ⑧/⑥

Sensory · Rot

Read the curses, debts, and secret shames that rot carries.

Limit: Visions arrive as accusations — you see yourself in every reading.

Name It: Spend a glitter die to ask one question about a curse, debt, or hidden rot in the scene; the GM answers honestly.

Workings, not spell lists

Anything bigger than one branch — thawing Frostgate, marrying the Blizzard — is a Challenge or Timed Test, not a bigger spell. Glyph discoveries and teachers are fiction; growth is mechanical. The storm doesn't care about your character sheet.

Option E · Magic as Power Sets

Cryomancy

The heroic treatment. A Power Set is a rated suite — each power rolls like a trait — bound by at least one SFX and one Limit that govern the whole set. A gifted character takes the Cryomancy set with two powers, one at ⑧ and one at ⑥, and grows them by die steps through play.

Set SFX — Shatter

Spend a glitter die to step up the effect die against someone suffering a Heartbreak complication. Usable with any Cryomancy power.

Set Limit — The Cold Takes

Whenever you include a Cryomancy power in a pool where the stakes are love or warmth, step up the Blizzard of Heartbreak after the roll. The frost always collects.

Why sets, plural

The Codex lets a character include one power from each of their sets in the same pool at no extra cost. Take both sets and the hybrid — the witch who learned the cold, the cryomancer who touched rot — rolls deeper than the purist. The mechanics reward the story Girlville wants to tell.

Option F · Magic as a Signature Asset

The Gift

One rated trait does all the work. A gifted character takes Cryomancy as a signature asset at ⑥. Its six facets are narrative permissions, not separate traits — name the facet you're working each time you roll the asset. The asset steps up with growth (⑥ → ⑧ → ⑩ → ⑫) and earns SFX along the way: Shatter at ⑧, Reflect at ⑩.

The disarm scene

Option K · Magic as Resources

Winter’s Favor

Magic as fuel, not a trait. Each session begins with Winter’s Favor ⑥⑥⑥ — the storm’s attention, measured out. When you work magic, commit any number of Favor dice: roll them separately, add the highest to your total. Committed dice are spent — they return at downtime, after a proper grooming montage, or when you feed the Blizzard.

What workings look like

The six familiar workings, one line each. No ratings; the Favor dice are the ratings:

  • Heartfrost — attack with emotional cold.
  • Glassveil — ward yourself in mirror-ice.
  • Snowblind — read the memories in the snow.
  • Deepdrift — cross impossible winter distances.
  • Glint — lend an ally supernatural shine.
  • Thaw — melt, mend, end enchantments. Rot-tagged: committing Favor to Thaw steps up the Blizzard of Heartbreak after the roll.

Feed the Blizzard

Step up or double a Favor die before you roll it. If the working fails, the Blizzard of Heartbreak steps up instead — or an extra die joins it. The storm remembers who fed it.

OPTION L
Magic as Narrative Permission

No rated magic · no spells, only glitter

✦  ❄  ✦
Option L · Magic as Narrative Permission

No Spells, Only Glitter

The leanest magic. No rated branches, no lists, no fuel. If the fiction says you can work ice — you’re a high winter elf at the Winter Prom; of course you can — you roll what you’d roll for anything: a Value, a sign, a Relationship, an asset. The effect die says what the magic does. Complications carry the weirdness: Frostbitten ⑧, Snow-Blind ⑥, Owing the Storm ⑩.

The standing precedent: the Snowflake sign’s Edit SFX already works ice magic this way. This option simply declines to build anything bigger on top of it.

Adjudicating a working

  • Set the stakes first: what does the magic cost if it fails?
  • Opposition is the Doom Pool, a Challenge pool, or a named GMC — never the weather as a character.
  • Let the effect die do the talking: ⑫ reshapes the scene; ④ barely frosts the glass.
  • Anything bigger than a scene — thawing Frostgate, marrying the Blizzard — is a Challenge or Timed Test.

What you trade away

No collectible branches, no rated growth, no glyph-hunting campaign. What you keep: nothing new to learn, and magic stays inside the interpersonal drama where Girlville lives. The storm is a metaphor that occasionally freezes people.
